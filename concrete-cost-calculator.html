Concrete cost questions

How much does a concrete driveway cost per square foot?

It depends on thickness, finish, base, reinforcement, tear-out and local prices. With this calculator's default prices, a plain 4-inch broom-finish driveway on a gravel base with rebar lands around $8 to $10 per square foot, and stamped and colored concrete around $15 to $19. Your local prices may be higher or lower, so treat it as a planning number and get quotes.

How many yards of concrete do I need for a 20x30 driveway?

At 4 inches thick: 600 sq ft × (4 ÷ 12) ft = 200 cubic feet, ÷ 27 = 7.41 cubic yards. Add 10% for waste and uneven grade and you get about 8.15 cubic yards. At 5 inches it is about 10.19 yards and at 6 inches about 12.22 yards.

Should a driveway be 4, 5 or 6 inches thick?

Four inches is common for patios, sidewalks and driveways that only see passenger cars. Many contractors pour 5 to 6 inches for driveways that carry trucks, RVs or delivery vehicles, or thicken the edges and apron. Check local code and your contractor's recommendation for your soil and use.

What is a short-load fee?

Many ready-mix suppliers charge an extra fee when a truck delivers less than a minimum amount, often somewhere around 4 to 5 cubic yards. Small patios and sidewalks often trigger it. The amount and the minimum vary by supplier, so ask yours. This calculator adds a default fee under 4 yards, and you can change it.

Is this calculator a quote?

No. It is a planning estimate built from default prices that you can change. A real quote needs a site visit to check grade, soil, drainage, access and local permit requirements.
